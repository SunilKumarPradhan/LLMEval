## 🎯 Overview {#overview}

> **What this session covers:** A deep, story-driven walkthrough of how **Knowledge-based LLM benchmarks evolved from 2020 to 2025** — starting from MMLU and branching out into reliability, human-exam-based, and depth-based benchmarks, eventually culminating in HLE (Humanity's Last Exam).

**Prerequisites:**
- Basic understanding of what LLMs are and how they're trained
- Familiarity with the concept of "model evaluation" (benchmarks vs. custom evals) from the previous session

**Why this matters:**
- When building real-world AI products, you must **choose the right LLM for the right purpose**. Understanding benchmarks helps you interpret leaderboard scores intelligently instead of blindly trusting a single number.
- Knowing *why* a benchmark was created and *what problem it solved* helps you understand its limitations — critical for making informed model-selection decisions in production.

## 🧠 Understanding the "Knowledge" Capability {#knowledge-capability}

> **Definition:** Knowledge capability measures **how much world knowledge an LLM retained during its training process** — i.e., how much information is "baked into" its weights and biases. This is also called testing the model's **parametric knowledge**.

### Why Knowledge is the MOST fundamental capability

- When LLMs were first trained on **massive internet-scale data**, the most basic expectation was: *"If I ask it anything from the internet, it should know the answer."*
- Other capabilities (Reasoning, Coding) are considered **emergent behaviors** — they appeared gradually as models scaled up, almost as a "side effect" of training.
  - **Reasoning** emerged as models learned to "think" step-by-step as scale increased.
  - **Coding** emerged because models were fed tons of code and eventually could generate programs back.
- But **Knowledge retention** was the **primary, intentional goal** from Day 1 of LLM training — hence it's the foundation on which the entire benchmarking story is built.

## ❓ Why Knowledge Testing Needed a Systematic Approach {#why-systematic}

**The naive approach (GPT-2/GPT-3 era):**
- People randomly asked models questions across various domains and informally judged if it "seemed to know things."
- **Problem:** Random question-asking gives no **quantifiable, reproducible measure** of how much knowledge a model actually has.

**The solution:** Build a **systematic, structured evaluation dataset** — this is exactly what MMLU did in 2020.

### The Story in Words

1. **MMLU (2020)** launched, becomes the most popular knowledge benchmark for years.
2. Over time (2020-2023), all major models (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Claude) score 80-90%+ → **MMLU saturates** (can no longer differentiate models).
3. This triggers **4 parallel research directions**:
   - **Direction 1 (Reliability):** MMLU doesn't check if the model spreads *misinformation* → **TruthfulQA** born (2021).
   - **Direction 2 (Human Exams):** Why invent new benchmarks? Use real human exams (SAT, Gaokao) → **AGI Eval** born (2022/23).
   - **Direction 3 (Depth):** MMLU only tests breadth (many easy questions) → what about deep, hard, research-level questions? → **GPQA** born (2023/24).
