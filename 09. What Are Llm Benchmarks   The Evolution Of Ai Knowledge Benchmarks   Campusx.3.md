## 🎯 Overview

This part of the lecture continues the **evolution story of LLM knowledge benchmarks**, picking up right after MMLU. It covers three major benchmarks:

- **AGIEval** – uses real human standardized exams (bilingual: English + Chinese)
- **GPQA** – tests *depth* of knowledge instead of breadth (PhD-level science questions)
- **MMLU-Pro** – an improved, harder version of MMLU designed to fix its flaws

> **Why this matters:** Every benchmark has a life cycle — it is born to fix a gap, becomes popular, gets saturated (models score too high, differences disappear), and eventually gets retired. Understanding this life cycle helps you correctly interpret marketing claims like "Model X beats humans in exam Y" — which are usually **overstated** and don't mean the model has surpassed human intelligence.

**Prerequisites:** Basic understanding of what LLM benchmarks are, MMLU (breadth-of-knowledge testing across 57 subjects), and terms like accuracy, zero-shot/few-shot, chain-of-thought (CoT), contamination, and saturation (covered in earlier parts of this lecture series).

## 🧠 AGIEval Benchmark

> **Definition:** AGIEval is a benchmark built by **repurposing real standardized human exams** (like SAT, LSAT, China's Gaokao, and civil service tests) to evaluate LLMs against genuine human performance data.

### 🔑 Key Differentiator
Unlike earlier benchmarks that *estimated* human performance, AGIEval used **real exam data** — actual humans took these exact exams, so there's a **proper human baseline**, not an estimation.

- Average human score: **67%**
- Top human (topper) score: **91%**

### 🌏 Another First: Bilingual Benchmark
AGIEval was the **first bilingual benchmark** — combining **English and Chinese** exams (roughly half-half), unlike prior benchmarks which were English-only.

### 📅 Timeline & Launch Scores (April 2023, right after GPT-4's release)

| Model/Group | Score |
|---|---|
| GPT-4 | 58% |
| ChatGPT | 43% |
| text-davinci (OpenAI) | 37% |
| Average Human | 67% |
| Top Human (Topper) | 91% |

> A **big gap** between SOTA model (58%) and top humans (91%) is considered a **good sign for a benchmark** — it means there's a lot of headroom left for models to improve, making it a good starting point.

### 📈 Life Cycle of AGIEval
1. **2023 launch** → wide gap between models and humans → good benchmark
2. **2023-24** → widely adopted; new models tested and reported scores here
3. **2024** → frontier models start closing in on the 91% top-human baseline
4. **2025** → benchmark **saturates**; models cluster near top scores; can't discriminate model quality anymore
5. Eventually **retired** — this is the standard life cycle for any benchmark

```
Benchmark Life Cycle:
Launch (hard) → Adoption → Frontier models improve → 
Approach human baseline → Saturation (all models cluster) → Retirement
```

### 📊 Dataset Details
- **20 exam sections** (20 papers), **8,000+ questions total**
- English papers: SAT, LSAT, LogiQA, AQuA, Gaokao (English version), etc.
- Chinese papers: Various Gaokao subject papers
- **Format:** 18 out of 20 exams = MCQ format; 2 exams = short-answer (generate the answer, not select from options)
- **Setup:** Zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) allowed
- **Core metric:** Accuracy (single average combining both languages)

### ⚠️ What AGIEval Does NOT Measure
This is a **critical insight** from the transcript:

> AGIEval only tests **knowledge for a specific exam paper** — it does NOT test:
> - Long-horizon tasks
> - Multi-step reasoning
> - Tool use

**Core insight:** Beating an average human test-taker on ONE exam **does not mean** the model has achieved human-level intelligence. A human (or agent) can do a LOT more than pass one exam — and this benchmark doesn't measure any of that.

## 📢 The Marketing Problem with Exam-Beating Claims

During 2023-24, headlines like *"LLM scored higher than humans in IIT JEE / CAT exam"* created fear that AI had **surpassed human intelligence**.

**Reality check:**
- These benchmarks only measure knowledge for **that specific exam**
- They don't test multi-step reasoning, tool use, or open-ended real-world problem solving
- Marketing at the time exaggerated these results to suggest "endgame" narratives, which was misleading

> 💡 **Takeaway:** "Beat humans on Exam X" ≠ "Surpassed human intelligence." Always check WHAT the benchmark actually measures before believing such claims.

## 🔬 GPQA (Google-Proof Q&A)

> **Definition:** GPQA (Google-Proof Question & Answers) is a benchmark that tests the **depth** of knowledge (instead of breadth) using PhD-level questions in Physics, Chemistry, and Biology.

### 🎯 Why GPQA Was Created
- MMLU had saturated
- MMLU tested **breadth** (57 subjects) but questions were mostly **easy** — even non-experts could answer many
- Researchers asked: *"What if instead of breadth, we test depth?"*
- Problem: Going deep in ALL subjects is expensive (expertise is costly) → so they picked only **3 subjects**: **Physics, Chemistry, Biology**

### 🏆 The "Google-Proof" Test
Questions are so hard that even a **non-specialist with Google access and 30 minutes** cannot solve a single question correctly.

### 📋 Key Facts
- **Launched:** November 2023
- Built by PhD-level experts using the **hardest science questions**
- **Every question validated by 2 domain experts** → very low error chance

### 📊 Three Dataset Subsets

| Subset | # of Questions |
|---|---|
| Main | 443 |
| Extended | 546 |
| **Diamond** | **198** (hardest subset) |

> When anyone reports "achieved X% on GPQA," they usually mean the **Diamond subset** (hardest 198 questions).

### 📈 Score Progression (Life Cycle)

| Year | Model | Score (Diamond) |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | GPT-4 | 39% |
| 2024 | GPT-4o | 56% |
| 2024 | OpenAI o1 (reasoning model) | 78% |
| 2024 | PhD Experts (hired by OpenAI) | 69.7% |
| — | PhD Experts (per original paper) | 81.3% |
| 2025 | Grok-4 | ~87% |

> ⚠️ Note the **inconsistency in PhD expert scores** (69.7% vs 81.3%) — this itself is a documented criticism of the benchmark (see below).

By 2026, GPQA is **near saturation** as reasoning-capable frontier models push scores above 80%.

### 📋 Task & Setup Details
- Questions: PhD-level, Physics/Chemistry/Biology
- Format: 4 options, select the best one (MCQ)
- Metric: Accuracy
- Run config: Zero-shot CoT allowed, Temperature = 0, pass@1, **no tool access**

### ⚠️ What GPQA Does NOT Measure
1. **General graduate knowledge** — it's strictly science-focused (Physics/Chem/Bio only); high score = good science absorption, nothing more
2. **Open-ended problem solving** — only MCQ selection, not generation
3. **Reasoning trace correctness** — only the final answer is evaluated, not HOW the model got there (could be correct reasoning, elimination, or even a lucky guess — all get equal credit)

### 🚩 Known Issues & Criticisms
1. **Very small dataset** — only 198 questions (Diamond) vs 14,000-15,000 in other benchmarks → lower statistical confidence (recall hypothesis testing / confidence intervals from statistics)
2. **"Beat PhDs" claims are inconsistent** — OpenAI's hired PhDs scored 69.7% but the original paper reported PhD score as 81.3% — so "beating PhDs" isn't a solid, agreed-upon fact
3. **Contamination risk** — benchmark is 2-3 years old; questions likely leaking into newer training datasets
4. **Narrow domain coverage** — only 3 subjects, not representative of overall graduate-level knowledge

> **Core theme:** GPQA pioneered the "depth over breadth" approach to knowledge benchmarking.

## 🚀 MMLU-Pro Benchmark

> **Definition:** MMLU-Pro is a direct upgrade/rebuild of MMLU designed to fix its known flaws, tagline: *"MMLU rebuilt to fix its flaws."*

### 🎯 Why MMLU-Pro Was Created
When MMLU saturated, different research groups took different directions:
- **GPQA folks** → chose depth over breadth
- **AGIEval folks** → chose to reuse real human exams instead of building new benchmarks
- **MMLU-Pro folks** → chose to **improve the existing good product** rather than build something new

### 🔧 Three Major Improvements Over MMLU

**1. Options increased from 4 → 10**
- With only 4 options, elimination strategy (eliminate 3, pick 1) is easy
- With 10 options, elimination becomes much harder → increases difficulty and reduces guessing success

**2. Removed trivia-based/noisy questions, added reasoning-based questions**
- MMLU was known for basic factuality-recall questions
