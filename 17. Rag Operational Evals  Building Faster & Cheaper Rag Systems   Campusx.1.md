> **Prerequisites**: Understanding of basic RAG architecture (Retriever → Context → Generator/LLM → Answer), familiarity with quality metrics like faithfulness, correctness, and answer relevance from earlier sessions.

**Why this matters**: A RAG system can give *perfect answers* but still fail in production if it's too slow, too expensive, or too unreliable at scale. Operational evals catch these failures **before** users complain.

## 🧠 What Are Operational Evals? {#what-are-operational-evals}

> **Definition**: Operational Evals for a RAG application answer a *different* question from Quality Evals (correctness, faithfulness, relevance). They tell you: **"Even if the RAG system gives good answers, can it run reliably, quickly, and economically in production?"**

In simple words:
- Quality Evals → "Is the answer *good*?"
- Operational Evals → "Can we *actually run* this system at scale without breaking the bank or making users wait forever?"

### Key Distinguishing Feature

| Aspect | Quality Evals | Operational Evals |
|---|---|---|
| Needs LLM-as-a-judge? | Yes (mostly) | ❌ No |
| Needs Golden Dataset? | Sometimes | ❌ No |
| Nature | Semantic/judgment-based | Software & telemetry driven |
| What it measures | Output quality | System behavior (speed, cost, uptime) |

> **Quote from transcript**: *"Operational evals are primarily software and telemetry driven. Unlike quality evals, they usually do not require golden datasets and LLM judges."*

This makes operational evals **simpler to compute** (just measurements/counters) but **equally critical**.

## ⚖️ Operational vs Quality Evals {#operational-vs-quality}

Think of it like buying a car:
- **Quality eval** = "Does the car drive smoothly and take me to the right destination?"
- **Operational eval** = "Does it start reliably every morning, how much fuel does it consume per km, and how fast can it go?"

Both matter — a car with a great engine but that breaks down every other day, or drinks too much fuel, is not a good production car.

## 🏛️ The Three Pillars of Operational Evals {#three-pillars}

Three main operational evals are covered in this series:

1. **Latency** – How long does the user wait for an answer?
2. **Cost** – How much money is spent per query?
3. **Reliability** – How often does the pipeline run successfully vs error out?

> **Note**: A 4th metric, **Throughput**, exists (how many parallel requests the system can handle) but is **out of scope** for this course because measuring it properly requires **Load Testing**.
